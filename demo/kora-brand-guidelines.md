# Kora Naturals — Brand & Content Guidelines (v2, 2026)

> Kora Naturals is a fictional skincare brand created for the Plumb demo. Any resemblance to a real company is coincidental.

## 1. Who we are

Kora Naturals makes gentle, plant-forward skincare in Bengaluru. We talk to people the way a knowledgeable friend would: warm, specific and honest. We would rather under-promise than overclaim.

## 2. Names and terminology

2.1 Our brand name is always written "Kora Naturals" — two words, capital K and capital N. Never "KORA", "Kora naturals" or "KoraNaturals".

2.3 Use official product names in full: "Aloe Calm Gel" (not "Aloe Gel") and "Rose Glow Serum" (not "Rose Serum").

2.4 Never describe a product as "chemical-free". Everything is made of chemicals; the phrase misleads customers. Say what the product is free from instead, for example "made without added parabens".

2.5 We are affordable, never "cheap".

2.6 Write "skincare" as one word.

2.7 We do not use "anti-ageing" or "anti-aging". Ageing is not a problem to fix. Describe the visible benefit instead, such as "for visibly smoother skin".

2.8 We never use fairness, whitening or lightening language. Kora Naturals celebrates every skin tone; words like "fairness", "whitening", "skin lightening" or "fair skin" are not permitted anywhere.

## 3. Voice

3.1 Plain words over jargon. Avoid corporate language such as "leverage", "synergy", "best-in-class", "cutting-edge", "revolutionary" and "utilize".

3.2 Never shame skin. Do not describe skin as having "flaws" or "imperfections", or call it "embarrassing" or "ugly".

3.3 Speak to everyone. Avoid gendered defaults like "ladies" or "girls" when you mean all customers.

3.4 Warm, never pushy. We invite rather than pressure. Avoid urgency and pressure phrases such as "Grab it", "Hurry", "Don't miss out", "Last chance" and "Act now". Good: "Find your new everyday gel." Bad: "Hurry, grab it before it's gone!"

## 4. Visual identity

4.1 Primary colours: Kora Clay #C8402A, Ink #15171C and Paper #FBF9F4.

4.2 Text placed over images must meet WCAG 2.2 AA contrast (4.5:1 for body text).

## 5. Claims

5.0 Every factual claim — a number, a statistic, a price, a feature — must match an approved fact in the Facts Ledger. If a claim is not in the ledger, attach evidence and ask the Claims team to add it before publishing.

5.1 Environmental claims such as "eco-friendly", "green", "sustainable", "natural", "organic", "biodegradable", "recyclable", "carbon neutral" or "plastic-free" must be specific and backed by an approved fact. Vague green claims are not allowed. This mirrors the CCPA Guidelines for Prevention and Regulation of Greenwashing or Misleading Environmental Claims, 2024.

5.2 We never make medical claims. Do not say a product "cures", "heals" or "treats" a condition, and never promise "no side effects" or "100% safe".

5.3 Superlatives and rankings — "#1", "best", "India's first", "number one", "leading", "most trusted" — need published evidence in the ledger. Without it, they cannot be used.

5.4 Prices, discounts and offers must match the current ledger entry. Expired launch prices must not be reused.

5.5 Statistics, customer counts and study results must match the ledger exactly, including the study size where one is given.

## 6. Accessibility

6.1 Keep copy readable: aim for a reading grade of 9 or below.

6.2 Do not use ALL-CAPS words for emphasis. Screen readers may spell them out letter by letter.

6.3 Use at most one exclamation mark in a piece of content.

6.4 Links must describe where they go. Never use "click here" or "read more" as link text.

6.5 Every image needs alt text that describes it.

6.6 Use no more than three emoji in a post. Screen readers read every one aloud.

## 7. Channels and disclosure

7.1 Influencer and creator posts must carry a clear disclosure tag — #ad, #collab or #sponsored — as required by the ASCI Guidelines for Influencer Advertising in Digital Media.

7.2 Respect channel limits: 280 characters on X, 2,200 on Instagram, 3,000 on LinkedIn and 160 for SMS.
